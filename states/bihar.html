Overview

Bihar, deriving its name from the Sanskrit word "Vihara" (monastery), is one of India's oldest inhabited regions. Situated in the eastern Gangetic plains, Bihar was the cradle of the Maurya and Gupta Empires — two of the greatest empires in human history.

It is the birthplace of Buddhism and Jainism. Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and Mahavira was born in Vaishali. Nalanda University, established in the 5th century CE, was the world's first residential university — attracting scholars from across Asia.

Bihar became a separate state on 22 March 1912. Today it is India's third most populous state, with a rich cultural heritage, vibrant folk traditions, and a young, rapidly growing population driving its economic transformation.

History

Bihar's history is among the most consequential in human civilisation — birthplace of two world religions, seat of the first pan-Indian empire, and home to the planet's oldest university. From Magadha's rise in 684 BCE to the modern republic, every era left an indelible mark on this ancient land.

684 – 321 BCE

Magadha — The First Superpower

The Magadha Kingdom, centred around modern-day Rajgir and Patna, was the most powerful realm in ancient India. Under the Haryanka, Shishunaga, and Nanda dynasties, it unified much of the subcontinent and laid the administrative foundations that Chandragupta Maurya would build upon. This same soil witnessed the Buddha's enlightenment at Bodh Gaya and Mahavira's renunciation near Vaishali — two events that would reshape the spiritual world.

321 – 185 BCE

Maurya Empire — Chandragupta & Ashoka

Chandragupta Maurya, guided by the legendary strategist Chanakya (Kautilya), founded the Maurya Empire from Pataliputra (modern Patna) — the world's largest empire of its time, stretching from Afghanistan to Bengal. His grandson Ashoka the Great transformed from a war-conquering king to a champion of non-violence after the Kalinga War, spreading Buddhism across Asia and erecting pillars of dharma that still stand. The Lion Capital at Sarnath — now India's national emblem — was his creation.

320 – 550 CE

Gupta Empire — India's Golden Age

The Gupta Empire, also based in Pataliputra, ushered in a golden age of mathematics, astronomy, literature, and philosophy. Aryabhata — who calculated the value of π and proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system — was born in Pataliputra. Kalidasa wrote his immortal epics during this era. Nalanda University was founded under Gupta patronage and became the world's foremost centre of learning, attracting students from China, Korea, and Central Asia.

12th – 16th Century

Medieval Period — Sher Shah Suri

Bihar fell under the Delhi Sultanate after Bakhtiyar Khilji's devastating raids (1193 CE) destroyed Nalanda. The region later produced Sher Shah Suri of Sasaram — the Afghan emperor who defeated Humayun, built the Grand Trunk Road (still in use today), introduced the Rupee currency, and reformed the postal system centuries before the Mughals. His octagonal tomb in Sasaram remains one of the finest examples of Afghan architecture in India.

1857 – 1947

Freedom Struggle — Champaran Satyagraha

Bihar was central to India's independence movement. The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 — Gandhi's first major civil disobedience campaign on Indian soil — was launched here against indigo farmers' exploitation. Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Bihar's own son, became independent India's first President. Bihar was carved out of Bengal Presidency on 22 March 1912, a date now celebrated as Bihar Diwas.

World Religions Born Here

Bihar holds a concentration of ancient sacred and intellectual sites found nowhere else on Earth. These are not ruins — they are living destinations drawing millions of pilgrims and scholars from across the world every year.

Gaya District UNESCO ★

Bodh Gaya

The holiest site in Buddhism — where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi Tree in 528 BCE. The Mahabodhi Temple (UNESCO, 2002) draws over 1 million pilgrims annually from 100+ nations. The original Bodhi tree's direct descendant still stands in the same spot.

Nalanda District

Nalanda Mahavihara

Founded in the 5th century CE, Nalanda was the world's first residential university with 10,000 students and 2,000 teachers. Its nine-storey library — Dharmaganja — housed over 9 million manuscripts. Scholars from China (Xuanzang), Korea, and Central Asia studied here for centuries. The modern Nalanda University has been revived nearby.

Nalanda District

Rajgir

Ancient capital of the Magadha Kingdom, sacred to both Buddhism and Jainism. The Buddha spent many rainy seasons here, and the First Buddhist Council was held at Saptaparni Cave after his passing. Famous for Gridhrakuta (Vulture's Peak), the hot springs of Brahma Kund, and the new glass-floor ropeway to the Peace Pagoda.

Vaishali District

Vaishali

Birthplace of Lord Mahavira and the seat of the Licchavi Republic — considered the world's earliest democratic republic (6th century BCE). The perfectly preserved Ashokan Pillar crowned by a lion stands here. The Second Buddhist Council was held at Vaishali, and the town is sacred to millions of Jain pilgrims worldwide.

Bhagalpur District

Vikramshila

The second great monastic university of ancient India, founded by Pala king Dharmapala in the 8th century CE. Vikramshila was renowned for Vajrayana Buddhist studies and produced the scholar Atisha Dipankara, who carried Buddhism to Tibet. Destroyed in the 12th century, its excavated ruins reveal a vast cruciform stupa complex on the Ganga's banks.

Patna

Pataliputra (Patna)

The ancient Mauryan capital — once the largest city in the world (c. 300 BCE) with an estimated 400,000 inhabitants. Greek ambassador Megasthenes described its magnificent wooden palace and vast fortifications in his Indica. Today, the Patna Museum houses the famous Didarganj Yakshi sculpture and numerous Mauryan relics excavated from beneath the modern city.

Geography

Regions

North Bihar — The Mithila Heartland

The fertile Gangetic plains north of the river, stretching to Nepal. Home to Mithila culture, the Gandak and Kosi rivers, and the litchi orchards of Muzaffarpur — which produce some of the world's finest litchi.

South Bihar — The Ancient Heartland

The historic Magadha region south of the Ganga. Contains Patna, Gaya, Bodh Gaya, Nalanda, and Rajgir — the cradle of ancient Indian empires. Drained by the Son and Punpun rivers, with the Rajgir hills marking its southern edge.

Champaran Region — The Western Plains

Bordering Nepal and Uttar Pradesh, the Champaran districts are known for their role in the 1917 Satyagraha, the Valmiki Tiger Reserve, and fertile sugarcane and wheat cultivation. The Gandak river defines its western boundary.

Angika & Kosi Belt — Eastern Plains

The eastern Bihar plains drained by the Kosi — known as the "Sorrow of Bihar" for its devastating floods — and the Mahananda river. Bhagalpur, famed for Tussar silk, anchors this culturally distinct Angika-speaking zone.

Rivers — The Lifelines of Bihar

Ganga

Flows 445 km across Bihar, dividing the state into North and South. Sacred, commercially vital, and the lifeblood of Bihari civilisation since antiquity.

Kosi

Called the "Sorrow of Bihar" for its catastrophic floods — it has shifted its course by over 110 km eastward in 200 years. The Kosi Barrage (1963) now tames much of its fury.

Son

A major tributary of the Ganga rising in Madhya Pradesh. The ancient Mauryans built irrigation canals along it. Rich in sand and minerals, it drains the Vindhya plateau into Bihar.

Gandak

Rising in Nepal as the Narayani, the Gandak forms part of Bihar's western border with Uttar Pradesh. The Gandak Barrage at Vaalmiki Nagar irrigates vast tracts of Champaran.

Major Cities

From the ancient Mauryan capital to the silk city and the makhana heartland

Capital

Patna

Ancient Pataliputra — City of History

One of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, Patna sits on the southern bank of the Ganga. Ancient Pataliputra was the Mauryan capital; today the city is home to the Patna Museum, Golghar granary, and Mahavir Mandir — one of India's most visited temples.

Gaya

Gateway to Bodh Gaya & Pitrupaksha

One of Hinduism's most sacred cities, Gaya is the centre of Pitrupaksha — the annual ritual for ancestors drawing millions of pilgrims. Adjacent Bodh Gaya, just 13 km away, is the most sacred site in Buddhism, with the UNESCO-listed Mahabodhi Temple.

Muzaffarpur

Litchi Capital of India

The commercial hub of North Bihar, Muzaffarpur is famous for its exquisite Shahi litchis — among the finest in the world with a GI tag. The city is also a centre for Makhana (fox nut) trade and the Mithila cultural heartland, known for its rich folk traditions.

Bhagalpur

Silk City & Angika Cultural Hub

Known as the Silk City for its world-famous Tussar (Bhagalpuri) silk — a GI-tagged fabric prized for its natural golden sheen. Bhagalpur is also the closest city to the ruins of Vikramshila University and a gateway to the scenic Ganga dolphin sanctuary.

Darbhanga

Cultural Capital of Mithila

The heart of Mithila culture and the Maithili language, Darbhanga was once ruled by one of India's wealthiest princely estates. The city is renowned for its classical music traditions, Sanskrit scholarship, and proximity to the Madhubani district — birthplace of the world-famous Madhubani painting style.

Nalanda

City of the Ancient University

Home to the UNESCO World Heritage ruins of Nalanda Mahavihara — the world's first university. The revived Nalanda University was inaugurated in 2024 near the ancient site, fulfilling a decades-long dream. Nearby Rajgir and the new glass-floor ropeway make this a major tourism corridor.

Culture & Arts

State Folk Dance

Jat-Jatin

Bihar's most beloved folk dance, performed by couples during the monsoon season. Jat (the husband) and Jatin (the wife) enact a playful lover's quarrel through graceful movements — a living celebration of the rain and rural life of North Bihar.

Folk Theatre

Bidesia

Created by the legendary Bhikhari Thakur — called the "Shakespeare of Bhojpuri" — Bidesia is a folk theatre tradition depicting the heartbreak of migration: a wife left behind as her husband seeks work in the city. Deeply moving, socially conscious, and musically rich.

Ritual Dance

Jhijhia

A beautiful ritual dance performed by young women during the Navratri season, with perforated earthen pots lit from within balanced on their heads. The dance is a prayer for rain and agricultural prosperity, originating in the Mithila region.

Folk Music

Kajri, Sohar & Chaita

The seasonal folk songs of Bihar mark every moment of rural life. Kajri songs celebrate the monsoon, Sohar are sung joyously at the birth of a child, and Chaita songs welcome the spring harvest — all sung in Bhojpuri and Maithili with great feeling.

UNESCO Recognised

Chhau Dance

A semi-classical masked dance form blending martial arts, acrobatics, and mythology. The Seraikela Chhau style originating near Bihar's Jharkhand border is UNESCO-listed as Intangible Cultural Heritage, performed with elaborate painted masks depicting gods, demons, and animals.

Classical Music

Thumri & Shehnai

Bihar's classical music heritage is immense — Ustad Bismillah Khan, born in Dumraon, Bihar, elevated the Shehnai to a concert instrument and received India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna. The Banaras gharana of Thumri has deep roots in Bihari musical traditions.

GI Tagged · Mithila

Madhubani (Mithila) Painting

Bihar's most iconic art form — originating in the Mithila region and traditionally painted on the walls of homes by women. Using natural pigments and bold geometric patterns, Madhubani paintings depict Hindu deities, nature, and wedding rituals. Now sold globally and taught as a fine art internationally.

GI Tagged · Bhagalpur

Bhagalpuri Tussar Silk

Woven from the cocoons of wild Tussar silkworms, Bhagalpuri silk is prized for its natural golden lustre and coarse texture. GI-tagged and exported worldwide, it is used for sarees, suits, and furnishings — a cornerstone of Bihar's handloom economy for centuries.

GI Tagged · Bhusura

Sujani Embroidery

A unique quilting and embroidery tradition from the Bhusura village in Muzaffarpur — women stitch old saris into narrative quilts depicting myths, weddings, and daily life. Promoted internationally by NGO Ekta, Sujani has become a powerful symbol of women's artisanship in Bihar.

Mithila Region

Sikki Grass Craft

Exclusively a women's art form from Mithila — intricate baskets, dolls, and decorative items woven from golden Sikki grass. Each piece takes days to complete and features geometric Mithila patterns. Sikki objects are traditionally gifted at weddings and passed down as heirlooms.

Ang Region · Bhagalpur

Manjusha Art

A 1,500-year-old scroll painting tradition unique to the Ang region of Bhagalpur, depicting the legend of Bishari Devi and Bihula-Balahaari through continuous serpentine narrative borders. Often called Bihar's "own Warli" — bold outlines, flat colours, and snake motifs are its signature.

Patna · GI Tagged

Tikuli Art

An ancient decorative art from Patna, originally used to make the bindi (forehead dot) worn by women. Tikuli art involves painting on lacquered glass with gold leaf and vibrant colours — depicting Madhubani motifs, Patna court scenes, and floral patterns. A GI-tagged craft being revived by artisans across Patna.

14th–15th Century

Vidyapati

Called the "Shakespeare of Maithili," Vidyapati's love poems — composed in the 14th century CE — remain sung and recited across Bihar and Nepal to this day. His Padavali poems on Radha-Krishna are among the most lyrical in any Indian language, shaping Maithili as a literary tradition.

Rashtra Kavi

Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar'

Born in Simaria, Bihar, Dinkar is one of the greatest Hindi poets of the 20th century — honoured as the Rashtra Kavi (National Poet). His epic Rashmirathi and Kurukshetra are masterworks of fire and patriotism. He won the Sahitya Akademi Award and the Jnanpith — India's highest literary honour.

Bhojpuri Shakespeare

Bhikhari Thakur

The "Shakespeare of Bhojpuri," Bhikhari Thakur (1887–1971) of Saran district created the Nautanki-style Bidesia theatre tradition — plays performed on village stages that tackled migration, caste oppression, and women's rights when no one else dared. His legacy is a testament to the power of folk art as social reform.

8th Schedule Language

Maithili Language

One of India's 22 scheduled languages, Maithili has its own ancient script — Tirhuta (Mithilakshar) — and a continuous literary tradition stretching back to Vidyapati's 14th-century poetry. Spoken by 35+ million people across Bihar and Nepal, it is the language of Mithila's rich intellectual and artistic heritage.

Sahitya Akademi Award

Phanishwarnath 'Renu'

Born in Araria, Bihar, Renu's landmark Hindi novel Maila Aanchal (1954) is considered one of the greatest works of post-independence Indian literature — a vivid portrait of rural Bihar that captured the soil, struggle, and soul of the region with unsurpassed authenticity.

Ancient Heritage

Sanskrit & Philosophical Tradition

Bihar is the birthplace of Navya-Nyaya — India's school of formal logic and epistemology developed in Mithila between the 13th and 17th centuries. The Mithila tradition of Sanskrit scholarship produced Chanakya's Arthashastra, Aryabhata's mathematics, and centuries of philosophical debate that shaped Indian thought.

Languages & Literature

Bihar is one of India's most linguistically rich states — home to five distinct language traditions with their own scripts, literatures, and millions of speakers. From the Vedic Sanskrit of Nalanda to Vidyapati's immortal Maithili verses and Bhikhari Thakur's Bhojpuri theatre, language here is identity.

Official Language

Hindi

Bihar's official language and the medium of education and administration. Bihar has produced giants of Hindi literature — Dinkar, Renu, Harivansh Rai Bachchan (who spent formative years here) — and the state's Hindi-speaking belt has shaped the language spoken across northern India.

8th Schedule · 35M+ Speakers

Maithili

Recognised in India's 8th Schedule in 2003, Maithili is spoken by 35+ million people in North Bihar and Nepal. Its own script — Tirhuta (Mithilakshar) — dates back over a millennium. Vidyapati's 14th-century poetry is the cornerstone of its vast literary tradition.

50M+ Speakers Globally

Bhojpuri

Spoken across western Bihar, eastern UP, and by diaspora communities in Fiji, Suriname, and Mauritius, Bhojpuri has over 50 million speakers worldwide. Its folk songs — Kajri, Birha, Chaita — are among India's richest oral traditions. A thriving Bhojpuri cinema industry also operates from Patna and Mumbai.

Language of Magadha

Magahi

Descended from Magadhi Prakrit — the very language in which the Buddha and Ashoka spoke — Magahi is spoken across the Magadha region of central and south Bihar. With 13+ million speakers, it carries the linguistic legacy of one of history's greatest empires and has a growing modern literary movement.

Ang Region · Bhagalpur

Angika

Spoken in the ancient Ang kingdom region (Bhagalpur, Banka, Munger), Angika has a distinct identity tied to the land of Karna from the Mahabharata. With over 7 million speakers and growing literary output, there is an active movement to include it in India's 8th Schedule of recognised languages.

Multilingual Heritage

Urdu & Sanskrit

Urdu is Bihar's second official language, with a rich poetic tradition in cities like Patna and Gaya. Sanskrit remains the language of Bihar's ancient intellectual heritage — from the Arthashastra to Nalanda's philosophical texts. Bihar produces some of India's highest numbers of Sanskrit scholars each year.

Tribal Heritage & Folk Art

Bihar is home to significant Adivasi communities — Santhal, Oraon, Munda, and Bedia among others — concentrated in the Jharkhand-border districts of Jamui, Banka, Munger, and Kaimur. Their art, language, rituals, and forest wisdom form a vital and irreplaceable layer of Bihar's cultural identity.

Harvest Festival Art

Sohrai Painting

A ritual wall-painting tradition of the Santhal, Oraon, and Munda communities, created during the Sohrai harvest festival. Using natural earth pigments — ochre, white clay, manganese black — women paint intricate animals, foliage, and geometric motifs on their home walls. Now exhibited internationally and sold as a folk art form.

India's Largest Tribe

Santhal Community

The Santhal are India's third-largest tribal group, with a significant population in Bihar's Banka, Jamui, and Munger districts. They have their own script (Ol Chiki), a rich oral literature, and distinctive music and dance traditions. The 1855 Santhal Rebellion — a pioneering anti-colonial uprising — was led from this very region.

Gaya & Rohtas Hills

Oraon Community

The Oraon (Kurukh) people are prominent in the hills of Gaya, Rohtas, and Kaimur. Their Karma festival — a joyous celebration of nature and the Karma tree — is one of Bihar's most vibrant tribal events, marked by all-night singing and dancing. Their Kurukh language has its own script and a growing literary tradition.

Jamui & Banka

Munda Community

The Munda of Bihar's eastern districts carry the legacy of Birsa Munda — the legendary tribal freedom fighter who led the Ulgulan (Great Tumult) uprising of 1899 against British land encroachment. Revered as Dharti Aaba (Father of the Earth), Birsa Munda is Bihar's greatest tribal icon.

Living Craft

Tribal Crafts & Bamboo Work

Bihar's tribal communities are master craftspeople — weaving intricate bamboo baskets, mats, and ceremonial objects using techniques passed down over centuries. Tribal pottery, ironwork (by the Lohar community), and leaf-plate making are everyday crafts that carry deep ecological knowledge and artistic skill.

Living Traditions

Tribal Festivals & Rituals

Bihar's tribal calendar is rich — Sohrai (harvest), Karma (nature worship), Jani-Shikar (symbolic hunting), and Bandna (cattle worship) are celebrated with all-night song, dance, and communal feasting. Each festival reaffirms the tribal bond with the land, forest, and ancestral spirits.

Cuisine

Bihari cuisine is hearty, flavourful, and deeply rooted in its agricultural landscape — smoky Litti Chokha baked in dung-fire embers, protein-rich Sattu, delicate Thekua for Chhath Puja, and the world-famous Shahi Litchi. Simple in technique yet profound in taste.

Bihar's Soul Food

Litti Chokha

Bihar's iconic dish — round wheat balls stuffed with spiced Sattu, roasted over open coals and served with Chokha (mashed roasted brinjal, tomato, and potato with mustard oil). PM Modi famously served it at diplomatic dinners, giving this humble dish global recognition.

Bihar's Superfood

Sattu

Roasted black chickpea flour — Bihar's ancient protein powerhouse. Consumed as a cold drink in summer, stuffed into parathas, or mixed with mustard oil and spices as a dry snack. Rich in protein and fibre, Sattu has been a staple of Bihar's diet for millennia and is now being rediscovered as a modern health food.

Chhath Prasad

Thekua

The sacred prasad of Chhath Puja — a crispy deep-fried sweet made of wheat flour, jaggery, and coconut, pressed into decorative moulds with floral and geometric patterns. No Chhath celebration is complete without Thekua. It is also enjoyed year-round as a wholesome snack with a long shelf life.

Winter Comfort Food

Dal Pitha

Steamed or fried rice flour dumplings stuffed with spiced chana dal — Bihar's answer to dim sum. Eaten as a snack or light meal, they are a staple winter comfort food across rural Bihar, often served with ghee and a tangy tomato chutney.

GI Tagged · World's Largest Producer

Makhana (Fox Nuts)

Bihar produces over 90% of the world's Makhana — the seeds of the Euryale ferox water lily, harvested from the ponds of Darbhanga, Madhubani, and Muzaffarpur. GI-tagged and prized as a fasting food, roasted snack, and gourmet ingredient, Makhana now exports to 20+ countries and is valued at ₹6,000+ crore annually.

Festival Sweets

Malpua & Silao Khaja

Malpua — sweet fried pancakes soaked in sugar syrup, a Holi and Eid staple. Silao Khaja is a GI-tagged layered pastry from Silao near Nalanda, made of wheat flour and ghee with 20+ tissue-thin crispy layers — offered to pilgrims visiting Nalanda and Rajgir for over 700 years.

Tourism & Heritage

UNESCO World Heritage

Bodh Gaya & Mahabodhi Temple

The holiest site in Buddhism — where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment beneath the Bodhi Tree in 528 BCE. The Mahabodhi Temple complex (UNESCO, 2002) attracts over 1 million pilgrims annually from 100+ countries. Dozens of international monasteries — Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Tibetan — surround the complex, making Bodh Gaya a living, breathing centre of global Buddhism.

Gaya District · 528 BCE
UNESCO World Heritage

Nalanda & Rajgir

The ruins of Nalanda Mahavihara — the world's first residential university (5th–12th century CE) — are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Nearby Rajgir, the ancient Magadha capital, offers the Peace Pagoda, Gridhrakuta Hill, hot springs of Brahma Kund, and a spectacular glass-floor ropeway over the Rajgir hills. The modern Nalanda University, inaugurated in 2024, rises beside the ruins.

Nalanda District · 5th Century CE

Vaishali & Patna

Vaishali — birthplace of Lord Mahavira and seat of the world's first republic — holds the iconic Ashokan Lion Pillar. Patna (ancient Pataliputra), the state capital, offers Golghar, the Patna Museum (home to the Didarganj Yakshi), Mahavir Mandir, and the Har Mandir Sahib — the birthplace of Sikhism's 10th Guru, Gobind Singh.

Vaishali & Patna · 6th century BCE

Valmiki Tiger Reserve

Bihar's only Tiger Reserve — a 899 km² protected forest in West Champaran, bordering Nepal's Chitwan National Park. Home to Bengal tigers, one-horned rhinos, gharials, and over 250 bird species. The reserve is named after the sage Valmiki who composed the Ramayana in these very forests.

Festivals

October – November

Chhath Puja

Bihar's most sacred and distinctive festival — a 4-day worship of the Sun God performed at river banks and ponds at sunrise and sunset. Uniquely demanding: devotees stand in water for hours offering Thekua and fruits. Chhath has spread with the Bihari diaspora to every corner of India and the world, becoming a globally recognised cultural symbol of Bihar.

November – December

Sonepur Mela

The world's largest cattle fair, held at the confluence of the Ganga and Gandak rivers in Sonepur every Kartik Purnima. Dating back to Mauryan times, it was once where armies bought war elephants. Today it draws millions of traders, pilgrims, tourists, and artists across 2–4 weeks, with elephants, horses, camels, and a vast cultural programme.

November · Mithila

Sama-Chakeva

A unique festival of the Mithila region celebrating the bond between brothers and sisters, through the folk legend of Sama (daughter of Krishna) and her brother Chakeva. Women craft intricate clay birds and figurines, sing traditional songs at dusk, and culminate in a symbolic immersion. A living piece of Mithila's intangible cultural heritage.

January & March

Makar Sankranti & Bihar Diwas

Makar Sankranti (14 January) transforms Patna's skies into a sea of kites — one of India's most spectacular kite festivals. Bihar Diwas (22 March) marks the state's formation with grand cultural programmes across all 38 districts, celebrating Bihar's ancient heritage and modern aspirations with pride.

Economy

Bihar is among India's fastest-growing state economies, averaging 10–11% GDP growth in recent years. Historically an agrarian economy, Bihar is now diversifying rapidly — with surging remittance income, a makhana and litchi export boom, a new industrial corridor, and the revival of Nalanda University anchoring its ambition to become the education and agriculture hub of eastern India.

Key Sectors

Backbone of Bihar

Agriculture

Bihar's Gangetic plains are among India's most fertile. The state is the top producer of vegetables in India, a major rice and wheat belt, and the world's leading producer of Makhana (fox nuts). Shahi Litchi from Muzaffarpur, Jardalu Mango from Bhagalpur, and Katarni Rice are all GI-tagged specialities.

₹6,000+ Cr Industry

Makhana Economy

Bihar produces 90% of the world's Makhana from the wetlands of Darbhanga, Madhubani, and Sitamarhi. Once a local fasting food, GI-tagged Bihar Makhana now exports to the USA, UK, UAE, and Southeast Asia — driving a ₹6,000+ crore annual market that supports 500,000+ farming households.

Skilled Workforce Export

Remittance & Workforce

Bihar's young workforce powers India — Bihari labour builds Mumbai, mines coal in Jharkhand, and staffs factories across the country. Remittances flowing back home form a significant pillar of the state economy. Bihar also produces a disproportionately high share of India's IAS and IPS officers annually.

Bhagalpur — Silk City

Textiles & Handloom

Bhagalpuri Tussar silk — GI-tagged and exported to 40+ countries — is Bihar's pride in the handloom sector. The state also produces Sujani embroidery, Sikki grass craft, and Madhubani paintings for export. Handloom and handicrafts support over 1 million artisan households across Bihar.

Buddhist Circuit

Tourism

Bihar's Buddhist Circuit — Bodh Gaya, Nalanda, Rajgir, Vaishali — attracts tourists from Japan, South Korea, Thailand, Sri Lanka, and across Southeast Asia. The revival of Nalanda University and the new glass ropeway at Rajgir are fuelling a major upswing in international religious tourism and heritage travel.

Emerging Industry

Industrial Corridors

Bihar is developing industrial clusters under the Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy — food processing in Muzaffarpur and Darbhanga, leather goods in Patna, and textile parks in Bhagalpur. The Patna–Gaya–Bodh Gaya economic corridor is being developed as a heritage-tourism-industry integrated zone.

Regional Economy

Patna Metro Region

Administration, banking, trade, education. Bihar's fastest-growing urban economy with rising real estate, retail, and services sectors.

Mithila Belt (North Bihar)

Makhana, Shahi Litchi, Madhubani art exports, sugarcane. The agricultural powerhouse of Bihar with growing food-processing clusters.

Magadha Region (South Bihar)

Tourism (Bodh Gaya, Nalanda, Rajgir), mining (Gaya hills), and the emerging Patna–Gaya economic corridor.

Champaran Region

Sugar mills, forest produce, Valmiki eco-tourism, and Nepal border trade through the Raxaul–Kathmandu corridor.

Bhojpur–Rohtas Region

Cement industry (Rohtas), Son river sand mining, and the Sasaram–Dalmianagar industrial cluster with historical significance.

Angika Region (East Bihar)

Bhagalpuri Tussar silk, Jardalu mango exports, Ganga fisheries, and proximity to Jharkhand's mineral belt driving cross-border commerce.

Modern Achievements & Innovation

From producing the highest number of IAS officers in India to leading the world in Makhana exports and reviving the ancient Nalanda University — Bihar is writing a new chapter of achievement that honours its extraordinary past while building an ambitious future.

India's Civil Services Capital

UPSC & Civil Services

Bihar consistently produces India's highest number of IAS and IPS officers per capita. Cities like Patna and small towns across the state have a deep culture of competitive exam preparation — Bihari civil servants have served as cabinet secretaries, chief election commissioners, and ambassadors across the world.

Inaugurated 2024

Nalanda University Revival

800 years after its destruction, Nalanda University was reborn — inaugurated by PM Modi in June 2024 near the ancient ruins. Funded by an East Asia Summit consortium of 18 nations, it is designed as a net-zero-energy campus and a symbol of Asia's shared intellectual heritage rising from Bihar's soil once more.

GI Tagged · 40+ Countries

Makhana — Global Superfood

Bihar's GI-tagged Makhana has conquered global health food markets — exported to 40+ countries and featured in Western superfood lists. The government has set up dedicated Makhana processing clusters, and a Makhana Board is being established to promote and regulate this ₹6,000+ crore industry.

Rapid Development

Infrastructure Transformation

Bihar has seen dramatic infrastructure investment — the Patna Metro Rail, new Ganga bridges (Mahatma Gandhi Setu reconstruction), four-lane highways connecting all districts, and a new international terminal at Patna Airport. The state's road density has more than doubled since 2005.

Education & Research

IIT Patna & AIIMS Bihar

IIT Patna (est. 2008) and AIIMS Patna are anchoring Bihar's transformation into an education and healthcare hub. NIT Patna, NIFT Patna, and Central University of South Bihar add to a growing cluster of national institutions that are reversing the brain drain and building local research capacity.

Global Diaspora

Global Bihari Diaspora

Bihar's diaspora spans the globe — from Bhojpuri-speaking communities in Mauritius, Fiji, Suriname, and Trinidad (descendants of 19th-century indentured labourers) to modern professionals leading tech companies in Silicon Valley and holding ministerial positions across the world. Chhath Puja is now celebrated on 6 continents.

Notable People

Founder, Maurya Empire

Chandragupta Maurya

Born in Pataliputra (Patna), Chandragupta founded the Maurya Empire — the first pan-Indian empire — in 321 BCE, guided by the genius of Chanakya. He united the subcontinent from the Himalayas to the Deccan and abdicated to become a Jain monk.

Emperor of Dharma

Ashoka the Great

Grandson of Chandragupta, ruling from Pataliputra. After the Kalinga War, Ashoka renounced violence and spread Buddhism across Asia. His Lion Capital is India's national emblem; his Dhamma wheel adorns the national flag.

Father of Indian Mathematics

Aryabhata

Born in 476 CE in the ancient Magadha region, Aryabhata was a student at Nalanda. He calculated the value of π to 4 decimal places, explained lunar eclipses scientifically, and correctly stated the Earth rotates on its own axis — 1,000 years before Copernicus.

First President of India

Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Born in Ziradei, Bihar, Dr. Prasad was India's first President (1950–1962) and a key figure in the independence movement. A close associate of Gandhi, he was also President of the Constituent Assembly that drafted the Indian Constitution. Bharat Ratna awardee.

Loknayak · People's Hero

Jayaprakash Narayan

Born in Sitabdiara, Bihar, "JP" led India's independence struggle alongside Gandhi and then the 1974 Total Revolution movement against Indira Gandhi's Emergency — one of the most significant democratic uprisings in post-independence India. Bharat Ratna awardee.

Rashtra Kavi · Jnanpith

Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar'

Born in Simaria, Bihar, Dinkar is revered as the Rashtra Kavi (National Poet) of India. His fiery epics Rashmirathi and Kurukshetra galvanised a generation. Winner of the Sahitya Akademi Award and the Jnanpith — India's highest literary honour.

Bharat Ratna · Shehnai Maestro

Ustad Bismillah Khan

Born in Dumraon, Bihar, Bismillah Khan elevated the Shehnai from a folk instrument to a concert stage classic. He performed at the Red Fort on Independence Day 1947, setting the tone for a free India. Recipient of the Bharat Ratna — India's highest civilian honour.

Master Strategist · Economist

Chanakya (Kautilya)

The greatest political strategist and economist of the ancient world, born in the Magadha region. His Arthashastra — a treatise on statecraft, economics, and military strategy written in Pataliputra — remains a masterwork studied in management schools worldwide today.

Builder of Grand Trunk Road

Sher Shah Suri

Born in Sasaram, Bihar, Sher Shah Suri defeated the Mughal emperor Humayun and built the Sur Empire. He built the Grand Trunk Road from Bengal to Kabul, introduced the Rupee currency, and established a postal system — administrative reforms so brilliant that Akbar later adopted them wholesale.

Sports & Games

Bihar has a deep-rooted sporting culture — from the ancient akhada wrestling tradition of Champaran to the chess prodigies of Patna and a cricketing legacy that produced one of India's most celebrated captains. The state punches well above its weight on the national sporting stage.

Moin-ul-Haq Stadium

Cricket

Bihar is the birthplace of India's most celebrated cricket captain — MS Dhoni was born in Ranchi (then Bihar, before the Jharkhand split). Patna's Moin-ul-Haq Stadium hosts domestic and IPL matches. Bihar Cricket Association is actively developing grassroots talent across all 38 districts.

Deep Rural Roots

Kabaddi

One of Bihar's most popular traditional sports, played in every village across the state. Bihar has consistently produced national-level Kabaddi players who have represented India internationally. The sport's deep rural culture has been a foundation for Pro Kabaddi League talent pipelines.

Akhada Tradition

Kushti (Wrestling)

Bihar has a centuries-old akhada (wrestling school) tradition, particularly in the Champaran, Muzaffarpur, and Bhagalpur regions. Traditional Kushti is practised in mud pits at hundreds of village akhadas — a living martial heritage that feeds into national freestyle wrestling competitions.

Ancient Heritage

Chess & Mind Sports

Chess — called Chaturanga — is believed to have originated in the Gupta Empire of Bihar and Bengal in the 6th century CE. Bihar today nurtures a strong chess culture at school and district level, with Patna hosting state and national tournaments and producing multiple FIDE-rated players.

Growing Grassroots

Football & Hockey

Football has strong grassroots support in Bihar, particularly in the Champaran and Muzaffarpur districts. Hockey is played widely in rural areas with significant SAI talent identification centres active across the state, producing players for national junior and senior programmes.

Track & Combat

Athletics & Combat Sports

Bihar's athletes have won national medals in athletics, boxing, and shooting. The state's Sports Authority infrastructure, including the Patna Sports Complex and residential academies, is nurturing the next generation. Bihar also has a strong tradition of Mallakhamb (gymnastics on a pole) rooted in its akhada culture.
