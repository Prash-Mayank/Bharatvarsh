The Ten Gurus

The spiritual lineage of ten human Gurus who guided Sikhism from its foundation to the establishment of the eternal Guru - the Guru Granth Sahib.

Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469-1539)

The Founder

Born in Talwandi (now Nankana Sahib, Pakistan), Guru Nanak founded Sikhism based on the principles of one God, equality of all humans, and devotional worship.

Divine Revelation

At age 30, experienced divine calling and proclaimed "There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim" - emphasizing the unity of all humanity under one God.

Extensive Travels

Undertook four major journeys (Udasis) covering thousands of miles, spreading his message across India, Central Asia, and the Middle East.

Core Teachings

Ik Onkar

One God - the fundamental belief in the unity and omnipresence of the divine creator.

Equality

All humans are equal regardless of caste, creed, gender, or social status.

Honest Living

Earn through honest work and share with those in need (Kirat Karo, Vand Chakko).

Naam Japna

Constant remembrance and meditation on God's name as the path to spiritual liberation.

Guru Granth Sahib

The eternal living Guru of the Sikhs - a unique scripture containing hymns and teachings from Sikh Gurus and saints from various religious backgrounds.

The Living Guru

Compilation

Compiled by Guru Arjan Dev Ji in 1604, later expanded by Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Contains 1,430 pages with hymns in multiple languages and musical ragas.

Universal Message

Includes writings from Hindu and Muslim saints alongside Sikh Gurus, demonstrating the universal nature of spiritual truth and divine love.

Eternal Guru

Guru Gobind Singh Ji declared it the eternal Guru before his passing, ending the line of human Gurus and establishing the scripture as the final authority.

Key Features

Musical Composition

Organized according to 31 ragas (musical modes), meant to be sung rather than simply read.

Multiple Languages

Written in Gurmukhi script but contains words from Punjabi, Hindi, Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic.

Diverse Contributors

Includes hymns from 6 Sikh Gurus, 15 saints, and 11 bards from various religious backgrounds.

Daily Worship

Central to Sikh worship, treated with utmost respect and reverence in all Gurdwaras worldwide.

Core Principles of Sikhism

Fundamental beliefs and practices that guide Sikh life, emphasizing devotion, equality, service, and righteous living.

Naam Japna

Meditation on God's Name

Constant remembrance and meditation on God's name as the path to spiritual enlightenment and liberation from the cycle of birth and death.

Kirat Karo

Honest Work

Earning a living through honest, hard work and avoiding exploitation or dishonest means of income generation.

Vand Chakko

Sharing with Others

Sharing one's earnings and resources with those in need, promoting community welfare and social justice.

Fundamental Beliefs

Ik Onkar

One God - the belief in one universal, formless, eternal creator who is present in all creation and accessible to everyone.

Equality of All

All human beings are equal regardless of race, religion, caste, gender, or social status. Everyone has direct access to God.

Rejection of Rituals

Emphasis on inner spirituality over external rituals, idol worship, or superstitious practices.

Service (Seva)

Selfless service to humanity as a form of worship and spiritual practice, helping those in need without expectation.

The Five Ks (Panj Kakar)

Kesh (Uncut Hair)

Maintaining hair in its natural form as created by God, representing respect for divine creation.

Kangha (Wooden Comb)

Keeping hair clean and tidy, symbolizing cleanliness and order in spiritual life.

Kara (Steel Bracelet)

Circular steel bracelet representing the eternal nature of God and reminder of righteous conduct.

Kirpan (Ceremonial Sword)

Symbol of duty to protect the innocent and fight against injustice and oppression.

Kachera (Cotton Undergarment)

Modest undergarment representing self-control, chastity, and moral purity.

Golden Temple & Gurdwaras

The holiest shrine of Sikhism and the network of Gurdwaras that serve as centers of worship, community service, and spiritual learning.

Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple)

Sacred Architecture

Built by Guru Arjan Dev Ji in Amritsar, the temple sits in the middle of a sacred pool (Amrit Sarovar) and is covered in gold, symbolizing spiritual purity and divine light.

Four Doors

The temple has four entrances facing all directions, symbolizing that people from all walks of life and all religions are welcome to enter and worship.

Continuous Worship

The Guru Granth Sahib is ceremonially installed each morning and continuous recitation (Akhand Path) and kirtan (devotional music) takes place throughout the day.

Langar - Community Kitchen

Free Meals for All

Serves over 100,000 free meals daily to visitors regardless of religion, caste, or social status.

Equality in Practice

Everyone sits together on the floor and eats the same simple vegetarian meal, demonstrating equality.

Volunteer Service

Operated entirely by volunteers (sevadars) who cook, serve, and clean as acts of devotional service.

Global Model

The Langar system has been replicated in Gurdwaras worldwide and serves as a model for community service.

Festivals & Traditions

Sikh festivals and traditions that celebrate the teachings of the Gurus, promote community unity, and reinforce spiritual values.

Major Festivals

Guru Nanak Gurpurab

Celebration of Guru Nanak's birth, marked by processions, community prayers, and special langar preparations.

  • Nagar Kirtan (religious processions)
  • Continuous reading of Guru Granth Sahib
  • Community service and charity
  • Fireworks and illuminations

Vaisakhi

Celebrates the formation of the Khalsa by Guru Gobind Singh Ji in 1699, also marking the harvest festival in Punjab.

  • Khalsa initiation ceremonies
  • Traditional Punjabi folk dances
  • Community fairs and celebrations
  • Renewal of faith and commitment

Hola Mohalla

Established by Guru Gobind Singh Ji as an alternative to Holi, featuring martial arts demonstrations and spiritual discourse.

  • Martial arts displays (Gatka)
  • Poetry competitions
  • Community sports and games
  • Spiritual discussions

Religious Practices

Daily Prayers

Sikhs recite specific prayers (Nitnem) at different times of the day, including morning, evening, and bedtime prayers.

  • • Japji Sahib (morning prayer)
  • • Rehras Sahib (evening prayer)
  • • Kirtan Sohila (bedtime prayer)
  • • Meditation on God's name

Gurdwara Worship

Community worship involving kirtan (devotional singing), prayer, and sharing of langar (community meal).

  • • Kirtan and hymn singing
  • • Reading from Guru Granth Sahib
  • • Community prayers
  • • Sharing of Karah Prasad

Life Ceremonies

Important life events are celebrated in the presence of Guru Granth Sahib with community participation.

  • • Naam Karan (naming ceremony)
  • • Anand Karaj (wedding ceremony)
  • • Amrit Sanchar (initiation)
  • • Antim Ardas (final prayer)

Modern Sikh Community

The global Sikh community continues to uphold the values of service, equality, and devotion while contributing to society in various fields.

Global Presence

Sikh communities thrive worldwide, maintaining their religious identity while contributing to their adopted countries' development and culture.

Community Service

Sikhs are known for their humanitarian work, disaster relief efforts, and community service, embodying the principle of "Sarbat da Bhala."

Military Service

Sikhs have a proud tradition of military service, contributing significantly to India's armed forces and serving with distinction in various conflicts.

Contemporary Contributions

Fields of Excellence

  • Agriculture and farming innovations
  • Business and entrepreneurship
  • Sports and athletics
  • Arts, music, and entertainment
  • Technology and innovation

Challenges and Adaptations

  • • Maintaining religious identity in diverse societies
  • • Educating others about Sikh beliefs and practices
  • • Preserving Punjabi language and culture
  • • Engaging youth in religious traditions
  • • Addressing misconceptions and stereotypes
