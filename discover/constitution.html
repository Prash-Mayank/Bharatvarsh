Overview

The Constitution of India is the supreme legal document that lays out the framework that defines fundamental political principles, establishes the structure, procedures, powers, and duties of government institutions, and sets out fundamental rights, directive principles, and the duties of citizens.

Drafted by the Constituent Assembly under the chairmanship of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, it took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days to complete. The Constitution declares India to be a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic, assuring its citizens justice, equality, and liberty.

Key Features

Longest Constitution

With 448 articles in 25 parts, 12 schedules, and 105 amendments, it is the world's longest written constitution, covering every aspect of governance.

Universal Adult Suffrage

Grants every citizen aged 18 and above the right to vote without discrimination based on caste, race, religion, sex, or education.

Fundamental Rights

Six fundamental rights guarantee individual freedoms including equality, freedom of speech, freedom of religion, and right to constitutional remedies.

Independent Judiciary

Establishes an independent judicial system with the Supreme Court at the apex, ensuring rule of law and protecting citizens' rights.

Fundamental Rights

Six categories of rights guaranteed to all citizens

Right to Equality

Equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, equality of opportunity in public employment.

Right to Freedom

Freedom of speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession.

Right Against Exploitation

Prohibition of human trafficking, forced labor, and child labor.

Right to Freedom of Religion

Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.

Cultural & Educational Rights

Protection of interests of minorities to conserve their language, script, and culture.

Right to Constitutional Remedies

Right to move courts for enforcement of fundamental rights through writs.

Making of the Constitution

The Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946 with 389 members representing the provinces and princely states. After partition, it was reduced to 299 members who worked tirelessly to draft the Constitution.

The Assembly held 11 sessions over 165 days, with Dr. Rajendra Prasad as President and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as Chairman of the Drafting Committee.

Fundamental Duties

Added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, these 11 duties remind citizens of their moral obligations to the nation.

1

Respect Constitution

Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, institutions, National Flag, and National Anthem.

2

Cherish Noble Ideals

Cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom.

3

Protect Sovereignty

Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.

4

Defend the Country

Defend the country and render national service when called upon.

5

Promote Harmony

Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood among all people of India.

6

Preserve Heritage

Value and preserve the rich heritage of the country's composite culture.

Additional duties include: Protecting environment, developing scientific temper, safeguarding public property, striving for excellence, and providing education opportunities.

Directive Principles of State Policy

Guiding Principles

The Directive Principles are guidelines for the government to create a just society. Though not legally enforceable, they are fundamental in governance and aim to establish economic and social democracy.

Inspired by the Irish Constitution, these principles direct the state to work towards the welfare of the people and establish a just social order.

Key Areas

Social Justice

Equal justice and free legal aid, prohibition of discrimination

Economic Welfare

Right to work, living wages, social security

Education & Health

Free and compulsory education, public health improvement

Environmental Protection

Protection of environment, forests, and wildlife

International Peace

Promote international peace and security

Amendment Process

Simple Majority

Some provisions can be amended by simple majority of Parliament members present and voting.

Examples: Creation of new states, citizenship provisions

Special Majority

Most amendments require 2/3rd majority of members present and voting, and absolute majority of total membership.

Examples: Fundamental rights, directive principles

Special Majority + States

Constitutional amendments affecting federal structure require ratification by at least half of the state legislatures.

Examples: Election of President, distribution of powers

Landmark Judgments

Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)

Basic Structure Doctrine

Established that Parliament cannot amend the basic structure of the Constitution. This landmark judgment preserved the Constitution's core principles from arbitrary amendments.

Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)

Right to Life Expansion

Expanded the interpretation of Article 21 (Right to Life) to include right to live with dignity, transforming fundamental rights jurisprudence.

Shah Bano Case (1985)

Uniform Civil Code Debate

Highlighted the need for a Uniform Civil Code and sparked national debate on personal laws and gender justice.

Vishaka Case (1997)

Workplace Harassment Guidelines

Established guidelines for prevention of sexual harassment at workplace, demonstrating judicial activism in protecting women's rights.

Global Influence

International Recognition

The Indian Constitution has been a source of inspiration for many countries, particularly in the areas of fundamental rights, directive principles, and federal structure.

Its comprehensive approach to balancing individual rights with social justice has influenced constitutional drafting in several developing nations.

South Africa

Adopted similar fundamental rights and directive principles

Nepal

Incorporated federal structure and judicial review concepts

Unique Features

Borrowed Yet Original

While borrowing from various constitutions worldwide, India created a unique document suited to its diverse society and federal structure.

Living Document

The Constitution's ability to evolve through amendments and judicial interpretation has made it a living document that adapts to changing times.

Democratic Success

India's successful democratic governance under this Constitution has been studied and emulated by many emerging democracies.

Did You Know?

  • The original Constitution was handwritten in both Hindi and English, with calligraphy by Prem Behari Narain Raizada.
  • The Constitution took exactly 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days to complete and cost ₹64 lakh (approximately $1 million today).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is known as the "Father of the Indian Constitution" and chaired the drafting committee.
