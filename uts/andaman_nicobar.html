About Andaman & Nicobar Islands

The Andaman & Nicobar Islands, a Union Territory since 1956, comprise 572 islands scattered across the Bay of Bengal. This tropical archipelago serves as India's easternmost frontier, strategically positioned between the Indian Ocean and Southeast Asia, making it crucial for maritime security and trade.

Known for their pristine beaches, coral reefs, and rich biodiversity, these islands are home to indigenous tribes with ancient heritage. The territory holds historical significance as the site of the Cellular Jail, where freedom fighters were imprisoned, and today stands as a symbol of India's struggle for independence while serving as a gateway to marine conservation and eco-tourism.
